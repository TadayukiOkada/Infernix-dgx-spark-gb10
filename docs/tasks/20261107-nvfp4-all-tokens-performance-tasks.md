## 2. Cold-L2 Operator Benchmark Audit & Identified Bottlenecks Across All Token Sizes

Using `infernix_linear_bench`, `infernix_q8_linear_swiglu_bench`, `infernix_hyper_connection_bench`, `infernix_gated_delta_net_bench`, and `infernix_qsa_attention_bench` with a 32 MiB cold-L2 flush on GB10 (`task-703`, `task-768`, `task-818`), we audited every operator across `T = 1..4096` against the user's 25 GPU kernel engineering principles.

### Bottleneck 1 (Fewer Kernel Invocations + Eliminate Temporary Buffers + Vectorized Tensor Core MMA): `proposal/head` `Q4 [131072, 2560]` Missing from `q4_dispatch.cpp` (`T = 1..8` MTP Drafter)
- **Root Cause**: [`src/ops/linear/q4/q4_dispatch.cpp:20-39`](file:///opt/etc/Infernix/src/ops/linear/q4/q4_dispatch.cpp#L20-L39) registers `131072 x {2048, 3072, 5120}` for `Q4_G64_FP16`, but **omits `131072 x 2560`** (`Qwen3.8-Flash-Next`'s `proposal/head`, `184.5 MiB`).
- **Consequence**: Because `linear_projects_head_rows(*head.rows)` ([`forward.cpp:127-131`](file:///opt/etc/Infernix/src/models/qwen4_exp/execution/forward.cpp#L127-L131)) calls `ops::linear_has_tuned_shape(Q4_G64_FP16, 131072, 2560)` and gets `false`, [`Forward::mtp_block`](file:///opt/etc/Infernix/src/models/qwen4_exp/execution/forward.cpp#L1530-L1539) on **every MTP draft step (up to 7 steps per decode round)**:
  1. Allocates a temporary `FP32 [131072, T]` workspace tensor (`524 KiB` at `T=1`),
  2. Runs generic SIMT [`ops::projection_fp32`](file:///opt/etc/Infernix/src/ops/projection_fp32/projection_fp32.cu#L915-L929) (`launch_tall`, without PDL), and
  3. Launches a separate `ops::cast_fp32_to_bf16` kernel (`524 KiB` read + `262 KiB` write) before `ops::top_logprobs` / `ops::argmax`.
- **Optimization (`A1`)**: Add [`src/ops/linear/q4/shapes/n131072_k2560.cu`](file:///opt/etc/Infernix/src/ops/linear/q4/shapes/n131072_k2560.cu) and register `Q4N131072K2560` in [`q4_dispatch.cpp`](file:///opt/etc/Infernix/src/ops/linear/q4/q4_dispatch.cpp) and [`q4_shapes.h`](file:///opt/etc/Infernix/src/ops/linear/q4/q4_shapes.h), sweeping GB10 GEMV (`T=1`) and Sliced-K MMA (`T=2..64`) schedules that write `BF16` directly with PDL. Eliminates 1 kernel launch and `786 KiB` of global memory traffic per MTP draft step (`up to 7x` per decode round).

### Bottleneck 2 (Async Overlap / PDL + Tensor Core Utilization): `text/output_head` `Q8 [248320, 2560]` (`636 MiB`) Bypasses PDL at `T = 1..32` and Uses SIMT at `T = 2..32`
- **Root Cause**:
  1. In [`src/ops/projection_fp32/projection_fp32.cu:915-929`](file:///opt/etc/Infernix/src/ops/projection_fp32/projection_fp32.cu#L915-L929), `kTallMinColumnsQ8 = 1` routes all `T = 1..32` calls on `Q8 [248320, 2560]` to `launch_tall` (`projection_tall_exact_kernel` at `T = 1..5`, `projection_tall_kernel` at `T = 6..32`). Unlike `projection_q8_kernel` (lines 930–938), `launch_tall` does **not** use `pdl::enter()` or `pdl::launch_consumer`, preventing the 636 MiB LM head from overlapping its weight preface with the final Hyper-Connection mixer!
  2. At `T = 2..32` (every speculative verification round of `5..16` tokens!), `projection_tall_exact_kernel` / `projection_tall_kernel` uses scalar/SIMT dot products instead of Tensor Core Sliced-K MMA (`launch_q8_a16_sliced_logits`, which is already implemented and used by [`n248320_k5120.cu`](file:///opt/etc/Infernix/src/ops/linear/q8/shapes/n248320_k5120.cu) to write `FP32` logits directly!).
- **Optimization (`A2`)**: Add `pdl::enter()` (after initial weight loads) and `pdl::launch_consumer` to `launch_tall` in [`projection_fp32.cu`](file:///opt/etc/Infernix/src/ops/projection_fp32/projection_fp32.cu), and benchmark Tensor Core `launch_q8_a16_sliced_logits` for `T = 2..32` on `[248320, 2560]`.

### Bottleneck 3 (Maximize Data Reuse + Eliminate 40x Redundant Shared-Memory Quantization): W4A4 Narrow MoE `gate_up_kernel` Re-Quantizes `x` 40 Times per Expert Job (`moe_layer.cu`)
- **Root Cause**: In [`src/ops/offloaded_sparse_moe/cuda/moe_layer.cu:1489-1685`](file:///opt/etc/Infernix/src/ops/offloaded_sparse_moe/cuda/moe_layer.cu#L1489-L1685), each narrow W4A4 expert job (`count = 1..4` tokens routed to an expert, covering 100% of `T = 1..16` decode/verify and ~70% of medium-T jobs) launches `kGateUpCtas = 40` CTAs (`slice = 0..39`, 16 gate+up row pairs per CTA).
  - Inside `gate_up_kernel`, **every single one of those 40 CTAs** independently reads the BF16 `x` column(s) (`2560` elements = `5,120 B` per column), stages them in shared memory, computes the warp/block FP32 absmax reduction, divides by `scales.input_gate`, and quantizes 2,560 BF16 values to E2M1 (`quantize_column_codes` / `quantize_pair_codes` / `quantize_quad_half_codes`) — **repeating the exact same quantization 40 times per expert job** (`400` CTAs per layer at `T=1`, `3,200` CTAs per layer at `T=8`)!
  - Even when `scales.input_up == scales.input_gate`, 39 of the 40 CTAs per job duplicate CTA 0's work.
  - Notice that [`src/ops/offloaded_sparse_moe/cuda/wide_expert.h:293-297`](file:///opt/etc/Infernix/src/ops/offloaded_sparse_moe/cuda/wide_expert.h#L293-L297) **already allocates** `std::uint8_t* narrow_acts` in `ExpertsWorkspace` with the comment: *"every entry's x column quantized to A4 codes once per call, once per input scale (gate, then up), which the narrow gate/up kernels read instead of re-quantizing the column in all of the job's CTAs"*, and [`moe_layer.cu:1905`](file:///opt/etc/Infernix/src/ops/offloaded_sparse_moe/cuda/moe_layer.cu#L1905) already passes `layout.narrow_acts` into `launch_expert_pass(..., int /*columns*/, ActCodes* /*narrow_acts*/, ...)`, where both parameters were left commented out!
- **Optimization (`A3`)**:
  1. Implement a lightweight pre-quantization kernel (`narrow_prepare_x_a4_kernel`, 1 CTA per routed entry `e = 0 .. K*T - 1`, or 1 CTA per `(token, expert)` job `j = 0 .. num_jobs - 1` reading `expert_scales[job.expert]`) that quantizes `(gate, up)` `ActCodes` into `layout.narrow_acts` **once per job** (only `10` CTAs at `T=1` instead of `400` CTAs!).
  2. Have `gate_up_kernel` load the pre-quantized `ActCodes` (`1,280 B` codes + `160 B` scales = `1,440 B` instead of `5,120 B` BF16 + 3 `__syncthreads()` reductions + E2M1 conversion) directly into shared memory via vectorized `uint4` loads.
  3. Preserve `early_shared = true` in [`forward.cpp:1229-1231`](file:///opt/etc/Infernix/src/models/qwen4_exp/execution/forward.cpp#L1229-L1231) when `source.staging_slots == 0 && streaming == nullptr` so the shared expert stream continues to overlap `moe_route_dispatch_plan`.

### Bottleneck 4 (Optimize Thread Block / Grid Dimensions + Eliminate Wave-Quantization Cliffs): Hyper-Connection Mix (`97x` per Pass, `746.9 MiB` Weights) Across `T = 4..4096`
- **Measured Cold-L2 Data (`infernix_hyper_connection_bench` & `infernix_linear_bench`, `task-768` & `task-818`)**:
  - `T = 1`: `29.60 us/call` (`260 GB/s`) via `hc_mix_fused` (`2` kernels)
  - `T = 2`: `34.46 us/call` (`224 GB/s`) via `hc_mix_fused`
  - `T = 4`: `45.79 us/call` (`168 GB/s`) via `hc_mix_fused` — **slower than `T = 5` (`44.64 us`, `173 GB/s`) and `T = 8` (`44.57 us`, `173 GB/s`)**!
    - Look at the comment in [`hyper_connection_mix_fused.h:16-23`](file:///opt/etc/Infernix/src/ops/hyper_connection/hyper_connection_mix_fused.h#L16-L23): *"the fused SIMT mix wins while its per-column dot-product loop amortizes under the MMA linear - for Q8 weights to 3 columns (T=3 39.7 us against the composed route's 41.9; T=4 43.8 composed against 45.2 fused)"*, yet line 23 set `kHcMixFusedMaxColumnsQ8 = 4` instead of `3`! Setting `kHcMixFusedMaxColumnsQ8 = 3` makes `T = 4` (which is MTP's standard `draft_tokens=3` verification width!) faster by `~1.2 us x 97 = 116 us` per pass!
  - `T = 64 -> 128 -> 256 -> 512`:
    - `T = 64`: `53.58 us` (`144 GB/s`)
    - `T = 128`: `86.13 us` (`89 GB/s`)
    - `T = 256`: `137.27 us` (`56 GB/s`)
    - `T = 512`: `244.68 us` (`31 GB/s`)
  - Why does `hyper_connection_mix` degrade at `T = 65..4096`?
    1. **`W_down` `Q8 [324, 10240]` (`96x` per pass)**: In [`src/ops/linear/q8/shapes/n324_k10240.cu:24-33`](file:///opt/etc/Infernix/src/ops/linear/q8/shapes/n324_k10240.cu#L24-L33), at `T = 64` it takes `34.66 us (140.7 GB/s)`, then at `T = 96` jumps +59% to `55.14 us (100.7 GB/s)`, and at `T = 256` jumps to `104.26 us (85.7 GB/s, 16.29 TFLOP/s)` because `select_q8_n324_k10240` switches at `T = 130..640` to `launch_q8_a16_mma_r32_t32_k128_a1` (`11` row tiles of 32 rows), whereas `select_q8_n320_k10240` ([`n320_k10240.cu:34`](file:///opt/etc/Infernix/src/ops/linear/q8/shapes/n320_k10240.cu#L34)) uses `launch_q8_a16_sliced_r32_t48_w4_s1` up to `T = 384`! And at `T = 128..384`, `r32_t32` / `r64x32_t64` can be re-swept for `324 x 10240`.
    2. **`hc_up_collapse` `Q8 [10240, 320]` (`97x` per pass)**: Look at [`src/ops/hyper_connection/hyper_connection_up_collapse.cu:148-158`](file:///opt/etc/Infernix/src/ops/hyper_connection/hyper_connection_up_collapse.cu#L148-L158):
       - At `T = 33..48`, `hc_up_collapse` uses `MmaR64x16T48K128A1` even though line 142's own comment states: *"The 64-row K128 tile of the 17..48 band runs ~180 GB/s here and loses even to the composed pair it replaces"* (and `select_q8_n10240_k320` in [`n10240_k320.cu:33`](file:///opt/etc/Infernix/src/ops/linear/q8/shapes/n10240_k320.cu#L33) uses `MmaR32T24` at `T = 33..48`!).
       - At `T = 49..128`, `hc_up_collapse` uses `MmaR128x32T64K64A2` (`80` row tiles x `1` or `2` token tiles), while at `T = 49..64` `select_q8_n10240_k320` uses `MmaR64x32T128K64A2` (`160` row tiles x `1` token tile = `160` CTAs vs `80` CTAs!).
       - At `T >= 129`, `hc_up_collapse` switches to `MmaR128x64T128K64A1` (`80` row tiles x `ceil(T/128)` token tiles). At `T = 129..256`, `MmaR64x32T64K64A2` / `MmaR64x32T128K64A2` has `160` row tiles (`2x` finer row granularity and smaller shared-memory scratch `u[BN][BM+8]`: `64 * 72 * 2 = 9.2 KiB` vs `128 * 136 * 2 = 34.8 KiB`, allowing 2–3 CTAs/SM instead of 1 CTA/SM!).
    3. **`hyper_connection_norm` & `hyper_connection_inject_norm` (`97x` per pass at `T >= 5`)**:
       - In [`hyper_connection.cu:141-200`](file:///opt/etc/Infernix/src/ops/hyper_connection/hyper_connection.cu#L141-L200), `norm_vec8_kernel` and `inject_norm_vec8_kernel` launch `dim3(streams=4, columns=T)` CTAs with `320` threads (`10` warps). In `block_sum` (lines 39–48), **all 10 warps** execute a loop `for (int w = 0; w < 10; ++w) total += scratch[w];` followed by a **second `__syncthreads()`** that is completely unnecessary (`scratch` is never overwritten after line 43!). Removing the redundant second `__syncthreads()` in `block_sum` saves a barrier in every `norm_vec8_kernel` and `inject_norm_vec8_kernel` call (`97x` per pass!).
       - Furthermore, `norm_vec8_kernel` (called `49x` per pass in `hyper_connection_mix`) does **not** call `pdl::enter()` or `pdl::launch_consumer` (unlike `inject_norm_vec8_kernel` which has `pdl::enter()` at line 192!). Adding `pdl::enter()` (after norm weight load) and `pdl::launch_consumer` to `norm_vec8_kernel` overlaps `attn_hc` norm with the preceding layer's MoE combine / PLE!

### Bottleneck 5 (Kernel Re-Sweep + Fast-Path Gaps on Q8 Linear & Q8 LinearSwiGLU Across `T = 1..4096`)
From `task-703.log`, three Q8 operators that execute **49 to 98 times per forward pass** have severe schedule cliffs and missing fast paths:

1. **`Q8 [2560, 640]` (`49x` per pass: `moe/shared/down`)**:
   - Currently has **`fast = nullptr`** in [`q8_dispatch.cpp:60`](file:///opt/etc/Infernix/src/ops/linear/q8/q8_dispatch.cpp#L60)!
   - Because `select_q8_n2560_k640` ([`n2560_k640.cu:17-23`](file:///opt/etc/Infernix/src/ops/linear/q8/shapes/n2560_k640.cu#L17-L23)) is constrained by the 16-column prefix-cache exactness contract to use `launch_q8_a16_mma_r32_t32_k128_a1` across `T = 1..32`, it runs at only **`45%–52%` of DRAM bandwidth** (`12.13 us` at `T=1`, `14.11 us` at `T=4..16`, vs `7.19 us` roofline floor)!
   - Adding `select_q8_n2560_k640_fast` under `LinearRoutes::Q8SlicedK` (using `fast_gemv` at `T=1` and Sliced-K `r16_t8_w4_s2` / `r16_t16_w4_s2` at `T=2..32`) and fixing the `T = 257..384` cliff (`22.34 us -> 32.61 us`, `+46%` jump due to `r128x64_t128` launching only `20 x 3 = 60` CTAs = `1.25` waves of 48 SMs) saves **`~100–150 us` per decode/verify pass** and **`~500 us` at `T = 257..384`**.

2. **`Q8 [1280, 2560]` (`49x` per pass: `moe/shared/gate` + `up` via [`q8_linear_swiglu_plan.cpp`](file:///opt/etc/Infernix/src/ops/linear_swiglu/q8/q8_linear_swiglu_plan.cpp))**:
   - Measured in `task-703.log` (lines 413–430):
     - **`T = 40 -> 41` cliff**: `26.46 us (141.2 GB/s)` -> **`38.75 us (96.6 GB/s)` (+46.4% latency jump!)** when switching from `SimtR8C8` (`320` CTAs) to `MmaR32C64` (`80` CTAs = `1.67` waves of 48 SMs).
     - **`T = 256 -> 257` cliff**: `42.85 us (119.5 GB/s, 39.2 TFLOP/s)` -> **`65.38 us (78.4 GB/s, 25.8 TFLOP/s)` (+52.6% latency jump — `T=257` is 10.2 us slower than `T=384` at `55.17 us`!)** when switching from `MmaR32C64` (`320` CTAs = `6.67` waves) to `MmaR64C128` (`60` CTAs = `1.25` waves of 48 SMs, 25% tail wave efficiency!). Across 49 calls, that single cliff adds **`+1.10 ms`**!
     - **Fix (`B2`)**: Re-sweep `kFlashNextSharedRoutes` in [`q8_linear_swiglu_plan.cpp`](file:///opt/etc/Infernix/src/ops/linear_swiglu/q8/q8_linear_swiglu_plan.cpp) across `T = 1..4096` (including `DFlash2MmaR32C64K128`, `DFlash2MmaR64C80K128`, `DFlash2MmaR64C96K128`, and extending `MmaR32C64` through `T = 384`).

3. **`Q8 [2560, 2560]` (`2x` per MTP step), `Q8 [13952, 2560]` (`1x` per MTP step), and `Q8 [2560, 6144]` (`1x` per MTP step)**:
   - On `Q8 [2560, 2560]` (`task-703.log` lines 208–246):
     - At `T = 1`, `select_q8_n2560_k2560_fast` uses `launch_q8_a16_sliced_r16_t8_w8_s2_m3` (`32.61 us`, which is slower than `T = 2` at `31.58 us` and slower than `fast_gemv`!).
     - At `T = 128 -> 129`, latency jumps **`48.96 us -> 67.49 us` (+37.8% cliff, slower than `T = 256` at `63.33 us`!)** because `launch_q8_a16_mma_r64x32_t128_k64_a2` drops to `40 x 2 = 80` CTAs (`1.67` waves) instead of keeping `t64` (`40 x 3 = 120` CTAs = `2.5` waves).
   - On `Q8 [13952, 2560]`, `fast = nullptr` in [`q8_dispatch.cpp:47`](file:///opt/etc/Infernix/src/ops/linear/q8/q8_dispatch.cpp#L47); adding `select_q8_n13952_k2560_fast` with `fast_gemv` / Sliced-K speeds up MTP draft/verify attention query projection.
   - On `Q8 [2560, 6144]` (`task-703.log` lines 295–315):
     - At `T = 48`, latency spikes to `160.0 us` (`109.6 GB/s`, worse than `T = 64` at `118.6 us`!) because `launch_q8_a16_sliced_r16_t32_w4_s2` launches 2 token tiles (`320` CTAs) for 48 columns instead of switching to `r32_t48_w4_s2` at `T = 33..48`.
     - At `T = 384`, latency spikes to **`364.35 us` (worse than `T = 512` at `237.34 us` by +53.5%!)** because `launch_q8_a16_mma_r128x64_t128_k64_a1` launches `20 x 3 = 60` CTAs (`1.25` waves of 48 SMs).

### Bottleneck 6 (NVFP4 Small/Medium-T `Nvfp4A4Pair` Thresholds, Wave Quantization & L2 Thrashing Across `T = 17..4096`)
Remember the critical architectural fact established in Section 1: all 97 dense NVFP4 weights on `Qwen3.8-Flash-Next-Local-NVFP4-W4Dense.infernix` have `policy == LinearPolicy::A16Only` (`input_scale_divisor == 0.0f`) with `LinearRoutes::Nvfp4A4Pair` enabled. Thus they transition directly from **`select_a16`** (`T < pair_min_tokens`) to **`Nvfp4A4Pair`** (`T >= pair_min_tokens`).

From `task-703.log`, three concrete bottlenecks appear on the 97 NVFP4 dense projections:

1. **`T = 33..64` `pair_min_tokens = 65` Penalty on `NVFP4 [16384, 2560]` (`36x`), `[13952, 2560]` (`12x`), and `[12800, 2560]` (`1x`)**:
   - Look at the measured cold-L2 latencies across the `T = 32 -> 33..64 -> 65` transition (`task-703.log`):
     - **`NVFP4 [16384, 2560]` (`36x`)**: `T = 32` is `108.32 us`; at `T = 33..64` (`select_a16` `MmaR64T64`) it jumps to **`124.96..126.82 us`**; then at `T = 65` (`Nvfp4A4Pair`) it **drops back down to `120.64 us`**!
     - **`NVFP4 [13952, 2560]` (`12x`)**: `T = 32` is `98.08 us`; at `T = 33..64` (`select_a16`) it jumps to **`112.35..118.62 us`**; then at `T = 65` (`Nvfp4A4Pair`) it **drops by 16.4 us down to `102.24 us`**!
     - **`NVFP4 [12800, 2560]` (`1x`)**: `T = 32` is `92.00 us`; at `T = 33..64` (`select_a16`) it jumps to **`100.19..106.37 us`**; then at `T = 65` (`Nvfp4A4Pair`) it **drops by 8.4 us down to `98.02 us`**!
   - Why? On `[2560, 6144]`, `pair_min_tokens` was already lowered to `33`, but on `[16384, 2560]`, `[13952, 2560]`, and `[12800, 2560]` (and `launch_nvfp4_n16384_k2560_split` / `launch_nvfp4_n13952_k2560_split`), `pair_min_tokens` was left at `65`!
   - Lowering `pair_min_tokens` from `65` to `33` on these three shapes (after verifying `Nvfp4A4Pair` qualification at `T = 33..64`) saves **`~6–16 us` per projection (`~350–500 us` per pass)** across `T = 33..64`!

2. **`NVFP4 [2560, 6144]` (`48x` per pass) `T = 17..32` A16 Dip & `T = 257..384` `Nvfp4A4Pair` Wave-Quantization Cliff**:
   - At `T = 16`, `[2560, 6144]` takes `44.86 us (203.4 GB/s)`, but at `T = 24` it jumps to `53.09 us (174.5 GB/s)` and at `T = 32` to `59.20 us (158.9 GB/s)` — slower than `T = 33` (`57.18 us`, where `Nvfp4A4Pair` starts)! Re-sweeping `select_nvfp4_a16_n2560_k6144` at `T = 17..32` (or lowering `pair_min_tokens` to `17` if faster and qualified) recovers `~4–8 us x 48 = 190–380 us` per pass.
   - At `T = 256`, `[2560, 6144]` takes `77.63 us (171.4 GB/s, 103.7 TFLOP/s)`, but at `T = 384` it jumps **+56.8% to `121.70 us (127.6 GB/s, 99.3 TFLOP/s)`** (almost as slow as `T = 512` at `129.86 us`)!
   - **Root Cause**: In [`launch_nvfp4_a4_residual_tma_linear`](file:///opt/etc/Infernix/src/ops/linear/nvfp4/nvfp4_a4_tma.cu#L86-L95), the 64-row tile (`Nvfp4A4TmaMmaSchedule<128, 3, 1, Promotions, 2, 64>`) is only used when `ctas_128 <= 24`. For `N = 2560` (`20` row tiles of 128), at `T = 257..384` (`3` token tiles of 128), `ctas_128 = 20 x 3 = 60` CTAs (`1.25` waves of 48 SMs — 48 CTAs in wave 1, 12 CTAs in wave 2 = **25% tail wave efficiency**!). Using the 64-row residual TMA tile (`40` row tiles x `3` token tiles = `120` CTAs = `2.5` waves of 48 SMs) or a 64-token residual TMA tile at `T = 257..384` eliminates the 60-CTA wave cliff across all 48 output projections (`~1.0–1.5 ms` saved per pass at `T = 257..384`).

3. **`NVFP4 [2560, 6144]` (`48x` per pass) `T = 2048 -> 4096` L2 Eviction Order in `nvfp4_a4_row_scaled_residual_quantize_kernel`**:
   - At `T = 2048`, `[2560, 6144]` achieves `135.65 TFLOP/s` (`475.1 us`), because the 2-term quantized activation buffer (`2 x 2048 x 3456 B = 13.5 MiB`) fits inside GB10's `24 MiB` L2 cache.
   - At `T = 4096`, throughput drops to `124.84 TFLOP/s` (`1031.2 us`), because the 2-term activation buffer is `27.0 MiB` (`> 24 MiB` L2): [`nvfp4_a4_row_scaled_residual_quantize_kernel`](file:///opt/etc/Infernix/src/ops/linear/nvfp4/nvfp4_a4_quantize.cuh#L279-L365) processes tokens `0 .. 4095` in ascending `blockIdx.x` order, so by the time it finishes token `4095`, **tokens `0 .. 1023` have just been evicted from L2** — and then `nvfp4_a4_tma_kernel` (`GroupCols = 8` -> `1024` tokens per band) immediately starts by reading `group = 0` (`tokens 0 .. 1023`) from cold DRAM!
   - **Fix**: Map `token = written_tokens - 1 - blockIdx.x` in `nvfp4_a4_row_scaled_residual_quantize_kernel` (and `nvfp4_a4_quantize_kernel`) when `written_tokens > 1024`, so the last CTAs of the quantize kernel write `tokens 0 .. 1023`, leaving `group = 0` (`6.75 MiB`) 100% hot in L2 for the first TMA band!

### Bottleneck 7 (MoE Router `BF16 [513, 2560]` SIMT Fallback at `T = 33..63` & Under-Occupied Grid at `T = 64..256`, `48x` per Pass)
- **Root Cause**:
  1. In [`forward.cpp:1049`](file:///opt/etc/Infernix/src/models/qwen4_exp/execution/forward.cpp#L1049), `kRouterMmaMinColumns = 64`. For `T = 33..63`, `Forward::moe` calls `ops::projection_fp32`, where `kTallMaxColumns = 32` ([`projection_fp32.cu:916`](file:///opt/etc/Infernix/src/ops/projection_fp32/projection_fp32.cu#L916)) drops `T = 33..63` onto the generic `projection_kernel<128, 1, 8>` (8 columns per CTA, re-reading the `2.5 MiB` router weight 5 to 8 times!).
  2. For `T = 64..256`, [`ops::projection_fp32_router_mma`](file:///opt/etc/Infernix/src/ops/projection_fp32/projection_fp32_mma.cu#L37-L47) uses `Bf16A16TmaR64T64K64S3` (`64x64` tile), which for `N = 513` (`8` body row tiles + 1 scalar tail row) launches only **`8` CTAs at `T = 64` (`16.7%` of 48 SMs), `16` CTAs at `T = 128` (`33.3%` of 48 SMs), and `32` CTAs at `T = 256` (`66.7%` of 48 SMs)**!
- **Optimization (`C3`)**: Add a `32x32` or `32x64` TMA/MMA router schedule (`16` row tiles of 32 rows -> `16` CTAs at `T=32`, `32` CTAs at `T=64`, `64` CTAs at `T=128`) in [`projection_fp32_mma.cu`](file:///opt/etc/Infernix/src/ops/projection_fp32/projection_fp32_mma.cu) and lower `kRouterMmaMinColumns` from `64` to `33`.

### Bottleneck 8 (Gated DeltaNet Chunked Recurrence at `T = 8..15` and `T = 1024..4096`, `36x` per Pass)
- **Measured Cold-L2 Data (`infernix_gated_delta_net_bench`, `task-768`)**:
  - `T = 1`: `19.42 us` (`325.6 GB/s`, single-CTA-per-head `running` kernel)
  - `T = 8`: `37.57 us` (`174.5 GB/s`, `full_chunks=0, tail=0`, serial loop over 8 tokens)
  - `T = 12`: `46.78 us` (`143.0 GB/s`)
  - `T = 15`: `55.01 us` (`123.4 GB/s`)
  - `T = 16`: **`29.89 us` (`228.2 GB/s`, `full_chunks=1`) — `T=16` is 45.7% faster than `T=15` (`55.01 us`) and 20.5% faster than `T=8` (`37.57 us`)!**
  - `T = 1024`: `251.42 us` (`160.0 GB/s` logical, `544.8 GB/s` traffic; `14.75 MiB` workspace)
  - `T = 4096`: `1097.41 us` (`129.5 GB/s` logical, `482.1 GB/s` traffic; `59.00 MiB` workspace — `36x 1.10 ms = 39.5 ms` per 4096-token chunk!)
- **Root Cause & Optimization (`C4`)**:
  - Why is `T = 8..15` (`37.6..55.0 us`) so much slower than `T = 16` (`29.9 us`)? Because `kChunkSize = 16` only uses the parallel chunked recurrence when `T >= 16`, leaving `T = 8..15` (which is the exact token range of N-gram + MTP speculative verification rounds!) on the serial per-token recurrence loop! Allowing a padded/predicated single 16-token chunk (`full_chunks = 1` with predicated lanes `t < T`) for `T = 8..15` (when `batch == 1` and `verify == nullptr`, or in chunked prefill tails) or optimizing the `T = 2..15` verification recurrence saves `~8–25 us x 36 = 290–900 us` per pass at `T = 8..15`!
